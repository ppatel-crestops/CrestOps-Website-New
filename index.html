FAQ

What does the audit include, and what do we get at the end?

The audit covers automation, data quality, security and permissions, license usage, integrations, and reporting, and ends in a written report that ranks every finding by business impact with a cost to fix each one. It takes two weeks and the price is fixed before it starts. The report is yours either way; there’s no obligation to continue.

How is this different from hiring a full-time admin?

A loaded full-time admin costs $100K or more a year, and most 20-200 person companies have 10 to 15 hours a week of real admin work. The Run retainer covers that work at about a third of the cost, done by someone senior enough that you’re not training them on the job.

Do you work with our ERP?

Most likely, yes. The deepest experience is Salesforce with NetSuite, including Boomi-based integration, and the same approach carries to QuickBooks, Acumatica, and other ERPs. The audit maps exactly what data moves between your systems, in which direction, and where it breaks.

What happens after the audit? Are we locked into anything?

Nothing is locked in. The report prices each fix separately; you can hire CrestOps for the Rescue work, hand the report to your own team, or stop there. Most companies pick a handful of items rather than everything at once.

Who actually does the work?

Parth does. Audits and admin work stay with one person on purpose, so the context lives in one head. Larger build items go to a small bench of certified Salesforce developers, scoped and reviewed by Parth.

How do you handle system access and security?

Work happens through a dedicated user account that you create and can deactivate at any time; no shared logins. Substantive changes go through a sandbox before production, and access is removed the day an engagement ends. Anything specific to your compliance requirements goes into the statement of work.

What size and type of company is a fit?

The best fit is a 20-200 person product company, wholesale, distribution, or manufacturing, running Salesforce next to an ERP with no admin on staff. The same test applies to law firms and other organizations on Salesforce-based platforms: if nobody owns the org, the work is the same.
